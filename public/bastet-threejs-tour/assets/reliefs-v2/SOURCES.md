# Relief Sources and Reconstruction Notes

## Primary publication used for the Heb-Sed relief textures

Édouard Naville, *The Festival-Hall of Osorkon II in the Great Temple of Bubastis (1887–1889)*, London, 1892.

Public-domain scan used as the basis of the line drawings:

- Internet Archive item: `festivalhallof00navi`
- Heidelberg Digital Library also provides a digitized copy.

The project crops separate published plates/registers and converts them into:

- colour texture
- monochrome stone texture
- height map
- normal map
- roughness map
- alpha mask

These textures document imagery from Osorkon II’s festival monument. Their exact placement on the reconstructed walls is not claimed unless separately stated.

## Official archaeological overview

Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, “The Temple of Bastet.”

Used for the broad architectural sequence and royal phases, including:

- Osorkon I at/near the entrance.
- Osorkon II’s monumental Sed-festival gate and relief program.
- papyriform and Hathoric columns in the western section.
- Nectanebo II’s renovation of the sanctuary.

Official page:

`https://egymonuments.gov.eg/monuments/the-temple-of-bastet/`

## Museum comparanda

British Museum object EA1105:

- red-granite relief from Osorkon II’s Sed-festival gateway at Bubastis
- includes fish and fowl offerings, the king in a Sed-festival kiosk, divine/ritual figures, and hieroglyphic texts

British Museum object EA1077:

- red-granite relief showing Osorkon II and Queen Karomama before a deity
- traces of colour are reported

The museum objects support the use of red granite, festival subjects, royal ritual scenes, and restrained surviving-pigment visualization. They do not prove the exact wall locations used here.

## Nectanebo II sanctuary program

The sanctuary textures are schematic graphic reconstructions based on published descriptions of the Thirtieth-Dynasty sanctuary at Bubastis. Themes used include:

- king before Bastet and local deities
- encircling text/program bands
- divine and shrine inventories
- protective group associated with the Seven Arrows
- star ceiling

The exact figures, spacing, and wall placement in these digital panels are interpretive and are explicitly labelled as such in `manifest.json`.

## Asset-level provenance

`manifest.json` and `manifest.js` provide, for every texture set:

- asset ID
- title
- source publication/page
- intended zone
- certainty statement
- source ID for derived/contextual variants

## Colour policy

Pigments are deliberately muted and partial. They visualize plausible surviving traces rather than a fully repainted modern fantasy reconstruction.
